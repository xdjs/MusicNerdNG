### Music Nerd Roundtable + Showcase — ~8 min

Not only as events — as places where Music Nerd generates trust, source material, relationships,
and stories that wouldn't otherwise exist.

- What kinds of conversations happen when you intentionally put certain artists, producers,
  curators, and listeners in the same room?
  *— Less what kinds of conversations happen, more: how do we capture, store, synthesize, and
  display the outputs of these conversations?*
- What stories emerge from a roundtable that an interview never would?
- Could those conversations become source material attached back to artists, songs, scenes,
  places, and relationships in the database?
- Could a showcase reveal relationships through performance and curation rather than through
  explanatory editorial?
- What should Music Nerd capture before, during, and after these gatherings?

The e-flux / *Becoming Each Other's School* piece is useful here not because Music Nerd should
become a school, but because it frames self-organized gatherings as sites where knowledge gets
produced. So: what knowledge and stories only become visible because we created the conditions
for people to be together — and how does Music Nerd preserve and make them explorable afterward?

## 3 · From relational database → things people can actually explore — 0:35–0:43

**⚠ Never reached. Carries to the next meeting.**

We've established that scenes should emerge through relationships, not genre tags or geography.
So how do we actually represent that?

**What constitutes an edge?** Collaboration · influence · shared bill · shared producer · shared
studio · label · collective · friendship · sample · remix · tour · co-sign · geographic overlap ·
shared movement or aesthetic · something an artist explicitly tells us.

- Do all relationships carry equal weight?
- How do we distinguish verified vs. inferred vs. artist-described vs. community-contributed?

**How should a listener experience it?** Rather than cramming this into the main site — what
small applications should exist on top of the database?

*Speculative, from GPT — don't put weight on it:* Scene Explorer (start at an artist, move
through their network) · Lineage (trace a sound or community backward and forward through people)
· Rooms/Circles (clusters around studios, collectives, venues, producers, moments) · Rabbit Hole
(give it an artist and let it build a navigable path through related stories, not "similar
artists") · Map of a Record (everyone and every relationship around one recording) · Who's Around
This? (a lightweight relational view on every artist and song).

The question isn't which feature sounds coolest. It's: **what interface makes relationship data
feel meaningful rather than looking like a generic network graph?**
