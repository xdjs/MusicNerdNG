### A big artist claiming a profile vs an underground one — ~8 min

Everything we have built and measured is against artists with thin, findable footprints:
Pete, Pharaoh, Dupes. Four to eight links, a handful of press mentions, one Instagram we
can read end to end.

A big artist breaks most of those assumptions. Hundreds of sources, most of them
low-quality aggregators. A feed of thousands of posts, run by a team rather than the
artist. Namesakes with real followings. Wikipedia and Discogs entries that are already
better than anything we would generate. And a claimant who may be a manager, not the
person whose captions we just read.

An underground artist has the opposite problem: almost nothing to find, and what exists is
a Bandcamp page and a friend's blog. The pipeline abstains a lot, which is correct and
also leaves them with an empty page.

- Are these the same product with different tuning, or two different flows?
- For a big artist, is our job to research at all, or to organise and verify what already
  exists?
- For an underground artist, is the interview the main event rather than the research?
- Who does the claiming, and does it change what we should ask?

**Goal:** decide whether the next sprint tests a big artist, an underground one, or both,
and name the artists.

### What to take from Genius, and what to avoid — ~8 min

Genius solved participation and then let it curdle. Worth being deliberate about which
parts we want.

What worked: annotation as a unit of contribution, IQ as visible standing, and lore as the
reason to come back — the meaning behind a song rather than the metadata.

What went wrong, and what it teaches:
- **Gatekeeping.** IQ thresholds gate annotating major songs, and remaining power users
  delete alternative readings to enforce a single fan head-canon. Fans complain about this
  openly on Reddit. A contribution system rewards whatever it measures, and Genius measures
  editing power rather than usefulness.
- **The pipeline disappeared.** When Spotify dropped Behind the Lyrics for Musixmatch,
  Genius lost the passive stream of listeners who fell into the lore from inside a
  streaming app. Their discovery surface belonged to somebody else.
- **Zero-click.** Google's AI summaries answer "what does this song mean" on the results
  page, so casual fans never arrive at all.

- Do we want contribution mechanics at all, or is artist-verified the whole point?
- If artists confirm their own credits and collaborators, do we need a fan reputation
  system — or does that reintroduce exactly the gatekeeping problem?
- Our lore is the artist's own words rather than fan interpretation. Is that a moat or a
  much smaller pond?
- MNTv is a distribution surface we own. Is that the Behind-the-Lyrics lesson applied?
- If AI summaries are going to answer the question anyway, is being the cited source the
  goal rather than being the destination?

**Goal:** decide whether contribution mechanics are on the roadmap at all, and if not, say
so out loud so we stop half-designing them.

### Music Nerd TV — pipeline and write-back — ~8 min

Integration is not the question; we know we want it. Two real ones.

**Does MNTv need a different research pipeline?** It answers "what is interesting about
this track, right now, while it plays" — timed, entertaining, disposable. Ours answers "who
is this artist" — durable, verified, slow. MNTv already searches with Exa, judges relevance
and defends against hallucination, all rebuilt separately from ours. It also resolves a
recommended artist by exact name and takes whoever has the most followers, which is the
namesake bug we spent a week fixing.

Correcting something I put in an earlier draft: **`/api/findArtistBySpotifyID` already
exists**, alongside `findArtistByIG` and `findArtistByDeezerID`. Identity lookup is not the
gap. I was wrong about that.

**How does MNTv's research get back into the Music Nerd database?** It researches artists
constantly and stores results keyed by `track_id`, so what it learns about an artist is
discarded at the track boundary and re-bought next time.

- One pipeline with two output modes, or two pipelines sharing an identity layer?
- What comes back — sources, or generated nuggets? (Writing generated prose into artist
  records reopens the contamination problems we spent this week closing. Sources entering
  the vault as pending go through judging we already trust.)
- Should MNTv call our research when it has no cache, rather than running its own?

**Goal:** decide the direction of the dependency, and what crosses the boundary.

### Also on the list, not scheduled

Raise if there is time, otherwise they carry:

- **Nothing we research is visible.** The knowledge document is edit-mode only. 50 of the
  487 accounts in Pete's Instagram feed are artists already in our directory and no screen
  shows it. What a collaborator view would actually be — a list on the profile, a filter on
  search, a graph — is undesigned, which is why it is not a topic yet.
- **Editorial control over an artist's own page.** Everything we extract is public: their
  posts, their captions. The question is not privacy but what belongs on a professional
  page. The extraction surfaces grief and burnout alongside credits because both are in the
  feed, and the artist should decide which appears — the same call any artist makes about a
  bio.
- **Almost every bug this week was found by an artist looking at his own profile**, not by
  tests. Two Pete Seeger reviews filed as Pete Rango's sources; an Instagram account whose
  display name is exactly "Pharaoh Sistare"; a Clubhouse scrape ranked beside real
  interviews. The common shape: silence meaning "we didn't manage it" read as "there's
  nothing there."
