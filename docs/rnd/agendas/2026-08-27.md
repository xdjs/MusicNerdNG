## 2 · Agenda
**0:05–0:32** · Pete leads

### Demo: claiming a profile, cold — ~8 min

Pete claims his own profile on staging from a completely blank slate: no links, no Spotify,
no Deezer, nothing but a name. Discovery runs live and finds his socials, the vault fills,
the interview asks him about a named collaborator from his own captions, and the document
is written from what it found.

Measured before the meeting: **10 of 12 known links found, 0 wrong, 0 namesakes** across
Pete and Pharaoh Sistare with everything cleared. The two misses are recall — it finds
less, it does not invent.

New this week and worth watching for: the interview question about **@p3t3rango mixing and
mastering Pharaoh's record**, pulled from a caption; source pills under every answer in the
ask; and the document citing Pete's own words, including his mother teaching him to make
empanadas.

Focus feedback on:
- Watching discovery run from nothing — does the waiting feel like work happening, or like
  a spinner?
- The interview asks about a specific person by name. Better or creepier?
- Where would you have stopped, closed the tab, or asked someone for help?

### What broke, and how we found it — ~7 min

Almost everything fixed this week was found by an artist looking at his own profile, not by
reading code or by tests. Two Pete Seeger album reviews filed as sources for Pete Rango. An
Instagram account whose display name is exactly "Pharaoh Sistare" adopted as his. A vault
where a Clubhouse scrape sat next to real interviews and Discogs was never searched for at
all.

The pattern underneath them is the same shape every time: **silence meaning "we didn't
manage it" being read as "there's nothing there."** An unreadable page returned no verdict
and was kept. An empty credits table looked identical to an artist with nothing to say. A
truncated model response looked like an artist who credits nobody.

- Where else are we treating a failure as an answer?
- We now abstain rather than guess in several places. Is a gap actually better than a wrong
  link, for an artist looking at their own page?
- Nothing in the UI had been clicked through before last night. What else are we shipping
  on measurements alone?

**Goal:** agree whether "find less, never invent" is the standard, and name one place we
should apply it that we haven't.

### The knowledge document is still invisible — ~6 min

We research an artist thoroughly and then show almost none of it. The document is edit-mode
only. The collaborator graph is not rendered anywhere — 50 of the 487 accounts in Pete's
Instagram feed are artists already in our directory and nothing shows it.

New this week: the document can be downloaded as a single markdown file with its sources
resolved, so an artist can hand it to another LLM. That is portability, not display.

- Who is the document actually for — the artist, a fan, or a machine?
- Some of what we extract is genuinely personal. Who decides what becomes public?
- Is the graph the thing worth building next, or the document?

**Goal:** a decision on what, if anything, becomes publicly visible on a profile.

### Music Nerd TV and a shared research API — ~6 min

Read MNTv's pipeline this week. It has independently rebuilt our research stack — same
searching, same relevance judging, same hallucination defences — and inherited our bugs:
it resolves a recommended artist by exact name and picks whoever has the most followers,
which is the namesake problem we just spent a week fixing. There is no connection between
the two products at all.

`/api/mcp` already exposes `search_artists` and `get_artist` with no auth. The missing
piece is lookup by Spotify ID, which MNTv always has.

- Should MNTv depend on us for identity, or stay independent?
- If it researches an artist, should what it finds come back to us? (Sources, not prose —
  writing generated text into artist records reopens everything we fixed.)
- Who owns that boundary?

**Goal:** decide whether to build the ID lookup, and who does it.
