### A big artist claiming a profile vs an underground one — ~8 min

Everything we have built and measured is against thin, findable footprints: Pete, Pharaoh,
Dupes. Four to eight links, a few press mentions, one Instagram we can read end to end.

A big artist breaks those assumptions. Hundreds of sources, most of them aggregators. A
feed of thousands of posts run by a team rather than the artist. Namesakes with real
followings. Wikipedia and Discogs entries already better than anything we would generate.
And a claimant who may be a manager, not the person whose captions we just read.

An underground artist has the opposite problem: almost nothing to find, and what exists is
a Bandcamp page and a friend's blog. The pipeline abstains a lot, which is correct and also
leaves them with an empty page.

- Same product with different tuning, or two different flows?
- For a big artist, is our job to research at all — or to organise and verify what already
  exists? Or could this just be handled by a research agent on the fly for Music nerd applications on top of database, storing as it finds data?
- For an underground artist, is the interview the main event rather than the research?

**Goal:** decide whether the next sprint tests a big artist, an underground one, or both —
and name them.

### What to take from Genius, and what to avoid — ~8 min

Genius solved participation and then let it decay. 

What worked: annotation as a unit of contribution, IQ as visible standing, lore as the
reason to come back — the meaning behind a song rather than the metadata.

What went wrong:

- **Gatekeeping.** IQ thresholds gate annotating major songs, and the remaining power users
  delete alternative readings to enforce a single fan head-canon. Fans say so openly on
  Reddit. A contribution system rewards whatever it measures, and Genius measures editing
  power rather than usefulness.
- **The pipeline disappeared.** When Spotify dropped Behind the Lyrics from Genius for Musixmatch,
  Genius lost the passive stream of listeners who fell into the lore from inside a
  streaming app. Their discovery surface belonged to someone else.
- **Zero-click.** Google's AI summaries answer questions like "what does this song mean" on the results page, so casual fans never arrive.

- Do we want contribution mechanics at all, if so how do we prevent gatekeeping? how does that scale? or is artist-verified the whole point?
- If artists confirm their own credits or lore, does a fan reputation system add anything — or reintroduce exactly the gatekeeping problem?
- Our lore is the artist's own words rather than fan interpretation. A moat, or a much
  smaller pond?
- MNTv is a distribution surface we own. Is that the Behind-the-Lyrics lesson applied?
- If AI summaries answer the question anyway, is being the cited source the goal rather
  than being the destination?
- Could we take a Dangertesting approach, and continue to deliver custom experiences for specific artists within our community? 

**Goal:** decide whether contribution mechanics are on the roadmap at all — and if not, say
so, so we stop half-designing them.

### Music Nerd TV — pipeline and write-back — ~8 min

Integration is decided. Two open questions.

**Does MNTv need a different research pipeline?** It answers "what is interesting about
this track, right now, while it plays" — timed, entertaining, disposable (we need to capture that data, currently we do not). Ours (Music Nerd Web) answers "who
is this artist" — durable, verified, slow. MNTv already searches with Exa, judges relevance
and defends against hallucination, all rebuilt separately from ours. (Need to test which pipeline is more cost-effective and accurate.)

**How does its research get back into the Music Nerd database?** MNTv stores results keyed
by `track_id`, so what it learns about an artist is discarded at the track boundary and
re-bought next time.

- One pipeline with two output modes, or two pipelines sharing an identity layer?
- What crosses the boundary — sources, or generated nuggets? Writing generated prose into
  artist records reopens the contamination problems we spent this week closing; sources
  entering the vault as pending go through judging we already trust.
- Should MNTv call our research when it has no cache, rather than running its own?

**Goal:** decide the direction of the dependency, and what crosses it.
