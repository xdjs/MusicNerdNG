## The onboarding demo

Pete demoed the flow end to end: claim profile → resolve Deezer ID → surface social links for
approval → add anything missed → show discovered web sources → ask a few questions generated
from the artist's recent posts → build a knowledge document with citations → generate the About
→ publish. Ran it live and on a recording.

Verdict: **ship it.** Carl — the direction of the flow is right, everything left is
optimization, and what exists now is good enough to ship. The remaining work is where to
prioritize making it better.

### Decisions

**Collapse the flow. One assertion, then the profile.** The current flow asks for a lot before
the artist sees any payoff. Target: assert a single data item ("is this you?"), then take them
straight to the profile, which we build best-effort without further input. Tweaking happens on
the profile page afterward. Carl's framing — minimize the chance of abandoning cart before
they've seen why they're doing this.

**The assertion should be a social handle, not Spotify.** Once Deezer resolves, Spotify is
inferable with near certainty, so spending the one question on it is wasteful. Ask for a social
instead.

**Pre-select confident matches; don't make people uncheck their own history.** Pete's proposal
to pre-check everything found and let the artist delete was agreed for the confident case. But
where several candidates surface for one platform — the multiple-Facebook-profiles case — CY's
point stands: unchecking your own old profiles feels worse than adding the one that's you. So
default-on for high-confidence single matches, manual add where the match is ambiguous.

**Capture the Spotify ID at artist creation, always.** Sleeve Note and Bopping both query by
Spotify ID. Any new artist added should get a best-effort Spotify ID resolution at creation
time, independent of onboarding. *(Concrete engineering requirement — not yet built.)*

**Artists should not edit Markdown.** Carl, on the knowledge document screen. The get-and-curate
problem and the present-it problem are separate; this screen currently conflates them. Ship as
is, revisit the presentation later.

**Say "Music Nerd," never "AI."** CY, on the "here's what the AI knows about you" copy. Talk
about the product, not the technology. Consistent with the existing brand-name rule.

**Test cold.** Carl: let artists hit it with no coaching and say nothing while they do. CY: take
every chance you get to put new eyes on it, don't overthink the setup — they surface concerns
you'd never have predicted. Pharaoh is scheduled for 8/21.

### Open threads

- **Two UI approaches for post-arrival editing**, to try and compare: (a) a wizard stepping
  through each section of the profile, (b) prominent per-section affordances the artist runs
  into while scrolling. No preference stated; build both and see which feels right.
- **Website links fail validation.** Pete pasted his own site during the "anything we missed"
  step and it was rejected. Separate question underneath it: an official artist website
  probably shouldn't be buried in the vault — it deserves surfacing further up.
- **Sources and citations in the knowledge document.** Pete wants provenance visible; the
  problem is that artist edits produce unreferenced lines sitting next to referenced ones, which
  looks broken. Carl suggested suppressing references; Pete wants to keep them. Unresolved.
- **Section quality is uneven.** "Career highlights" pulled things Pete wouldn't call highlights;
  "sound and influences" is the section an artist should have the most say over and is currently
  the most assumed. The section list came from the original Recoupable knowledge document and
  needs to be re-cut for what actually matters to us.
- **Follow-up questions over time.** The idea Pete is most interested in: a cron scans recent
  posts and emails the artist occasional questions about what they've been putting out, building
  the profile continuously instead of only at onboarding. Positive response from CY.
- **Platform coverage gaps** — Subvert didn't surface in discovery and had to be added by hand.

### The bar to design against

CY: an artist should come out of onboarding **feeling seen in a way they didn't realize they
could be perceived.** Put on the producer hat — find where the sparks are and help shape them
into the best narrative. Mature artists know exactly what they want to be; most independent
artists have delegated that and it isn't their priority.

Pete added a second axis: some education is needed inside the flow to nudge artists into caring
about this at all. The knowledge document asking "is this right, do you care about this" is
already doing a little of that work.

## Roundtable & showcase

Reviving something Jamie tried before that died on scheduling. Two formats:

- **Roundtable** — artists in a room talking. The value is the conversation an interview can't
  produce; formality imposes a certain behavior and this is meant to avoid it.
- **Showcase** — artists presenting what they're working on.

**Cadence: monthly each.** Roundtable at the top of the month, showcase at the end.

**Recording in Riverside** — higher quality, and this doubles as Music Nerd content. Pete to
check whether the free tier's limitations matter.

**Invites via the Music Nerd email list**, sourced from Jade. Approved.

The point isn't curation. It's that these conversations produce stories and context that go into
the database attached to artists, songs, scenes, and places — and once the data's there, other
things can be built on top of it. Nobody's telling anyone who to check out; artists tell their
own stories and we surface them.

Reference: the "becoming each other's school" piece — one place where artists show up to
showcase, run a workshop, or share what they're working on.

## Curated listening & world-building sites

Detail in [`../research/2026-08-20-curation-and-world-building.md`](../research/2026-08-20-curation-and-world-building.md).

Short version: Bandcamp Clubs and Cantilever both judged weak — the underlying concept isn't new
and the execution doesn't add anything. The useful conclusion is about positioning. Music Nerd
isn't trying to tell you what to listen to; it's helping you deepen a relationship with music you
already love. And curation only lands when there's a personal connection to the curator —
absent that, a system that knows your stated curiosities beats a generic tastemaker.

Separately, Jamie's new Sound of Fractures site opened a thread on how to convey the breadth of
an artist's body of work. CY: showing scope and letting someone drill into it are two problems,
and solving the first is the distinctive thing for Music Nerd to do early. Don't try to solve it
holistically.
